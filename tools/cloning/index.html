      ABOUT THIS TOOL

      How to use

      1. Pick the assembly: LIGATION for sticky or blunt ends with T4 ligase, GIBSON / HiFi for overlap assembly, GOLDEN GATE for a Type IIS one-tube reaction. The kit list sets the master mix and the incubation.
      2. Type each fragment's length in bp and its concentration. Nanodrop readings go in as ng/µL; a quantified library or an oligo duplex can go in as nM.
      3. Mark one row as the VECTOR. The ratio column starts at what the protocol asks for — a 2-fold excess of each insert for 2–3 fragments by HiFi, 1:1 for 4–6, 5-fold for an insert under 200 bp — and you can type over it.
      4. Set the amount either by the vector in ng (the way most protocols are written) or by the total pmol you want in the tube.
      5. Read the µL per fragment from the table. Anything in 3 · MIX marked in amber or red comes with the number that fixes it: a dilution, a larger reaction, or less DNA.
      6. Set the number of reactions and overage for a master mix, then copy or print the sheet.

      The formula

      pmol = ng × 1000 ÷ (bp × 650)
      bplength of the fragment in base pairs
      650average mass of one base pair of dsDNA, in daltons
      nMpmol/µL × 1000

      Volume to take = wanted pmol ÷ (pmol per µL). Each insert's wanted pmol = the vector's pmol × its ratio. Water = reaction volume − master mix (or buffer + enzyme) − every DNA volume.

      Example. A 5200 bp vector at 50 ng/µL is 0.0148 pmol/µL. For 50 ng (0.0148 pmol) take 1.0 µL. A 1100 bp insert at 25 ng/µL is 0.035 pmol/µL; at a 2-fold excess you want 0.0296 pmol, so 0.85 µL. With 10 µL of HiFi master mix in a 20 µL reaction, that leaves 8.2 µL of water.

      Good to know

      1. Free to use. Nothing you type leaves your browser. Your settings are remembered on this device.
      2. It does the amounts, not the design: overlaps, primers, Type IIS sites and overhang fidelity are a different job.
      3. Garbage in, garbage out: a Nanodrop reading on a dilute or primer-contaminated sample can be well off, and the moles follow the reading.
      4. A calculator, not a protocol. The insert in your tube behaves the way it behaves; the vendor's current protocol wins over this page.

      QUESTIONS · CLONING REACTION CALCULATOR

      Frequently asked

      How do I convert ng of DNA to pmol?

      pmol = ng × 1000 ÷ (bp × 650), taking 650 daltons per base pair of double-stranded DNA. 50 ng of a 5000 bp plasmid is about 0.015 pmol; 50 ng of a 500 bp fragment is about 0.15 pmol — ten times more, which is why equal masses are not equal moles.

      What insert to vector ratio should I use for NEBuilder HiFi or Gibson assembly?

      For 2–3 fragments NEB recommends a 2-fold molar excess of each insert over the vector and 0.03–0.2 pmol of total DNA for NEBuilder HiFi (0.02–0.5 pmol for Gibson). For 4–6 fragments it is a 1:1 molar ratio and 0.2–0.5 pmol total (0.2–1.0 pmol for Gibson). Inserts under 200 bp go in at a 5-fold excess.

      How much vector should go into a Golden Gate reaction?

      The NEBridge Golden Gate protocols use 75 ng of destination plasmid — 0.05 pmol for the 2155 bp pGGAselect — with each insert at a 2:1 molar ratio to the vector, in 20 µL with 2 µL of 10X T4 DNA Ligase Buffer and 1 µL of enzyme mix (2 µL for more than 10 inserts).

      How much insert do I add to a ligation?

      Start with 50 ng of vector and a 1:3 vector:insert molar ratio, or 1:1 if the two are a similar size. Short inserts need more: about 1:10 at 100 bp or less. Total DNA of 1–10 µg/mL in the reaction is the usual target.

      My fragments do not fit in a 20 µL reaction. What do I do?

      A 20 µL assembly leaves only 10 µL for DNA once the master mix is in. Either scale every fragment down, if the lower total stays inside the recommended range, concentrate the fragment that takes the most room, or run the reaction in 25 or 50 µL with the mix scaled in proportion. The calculator gives you all three with the numbers filled in.

      How much unpurified PCR product can I put in an assembly?

      NEB limits unpurified PCR product to 20% of the reaction volume — 4 µL in a 20 µL reaction. Tick the PCR box on a row and the calculator adds up those volumes and warns you when they go over.

      Does the calculator design the overlaps or check the restriction sites?

      No. It does the amounts: moles, ratios and volumes. Overlap and primer design, Type IIS site orientation and overhang compatibility are a separate job — the NEBuilder Assembly Tool and NEBridge Ligase Fidelity tools cover those.

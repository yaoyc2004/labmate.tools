    ABOUT THIS TOOL

    How to use

    1. One row per culture and interval: name or batch, the live cell count at the start (N₀) and at the end (N), and the time between them.
    2. Use total cells in the vessel — cells/mL × mL. Cells/mL alone is fine only if the volume didn't change.
    3. Logged the clock times? Switch to DATE & TIME and the interval is worked out for you.
    4. Passaged in between? Put the split in SPLIT × (4 for 1:4). Better still: count at the split and start a new row.
    5. Coming from Cell Count & Seeding? Fill in You have … mL there and press LOG FOR DOUBLING TIME — the first count starts a row, the next one ends it, with the clock times filled in.
    6. Tap a result to see the working. Several rows for one line (HEK293 · P14, HEK293 · P15…) give cumulative doublings and an overall doubling time for that line.
    7. Keep the record: CSV for Excel, SAVE RECORD to reopen it here later, or PRINT WORKSHEET for the notebook.

    The formula

    DT = t × ln 2 ÷ ln(N ÷ N₀)
    doublingslog₂(N ÷ N₀) = ln(N ÷ N₀) ÷ ln 2
    μln(N ÷ N₀) ÷ t, per hour; μ = ln 2 ÷ DT
    split ×sN is replaced by N × s
    ttime between the two counts

    Example. 100,000 → 400,000 cells in 48 h: fold 4, 2 doublings, DT = 48 × 0.6931 ÷ 1.3863 = 24 h, μ = 1.3863 ÷ 48 = 0.02888 /h. Doublings per hour, 2 ÷ 48 = 0.04167, is a different number — don't report it as μ.

    Good to know

    1. Free to use. No subscription. Nothing you type leaves your browser; the table is kept on this device until you clear it.
    2. Two counts give an average over the interval. They can't show whether the cells were in log phase — counts that include the lag after seeding or the plateau at confluence give a longer doubling time.
    3. If the cell number didn't go up, no doubling time is given. Numbers are never turned positive.
    4. The tool can't tell you why a culture grew slowly — medium, serum lot, density, passage number and contamination all matter.
    REFERENCE · DOUBLING TIME & GROWTH RATE

    Three numbers, not one

    The same two counts give three different numbers. They are easy to mix up in a methods section or a spreadsheet — the tool shows them side by side.

    QuantityFormulaUnit100,000 → 400,000 in 48 h
    Population doublingslog₂(N ÷ N₀)—2
    Doubling timet × ln 2 ÷ ln(N ÷ N₀)h24 h
    Specific growth rate μln(N ÷ N₀) ÷ t1/h0.02888 /h
    Doublings per hour (not μ)doublings ÷ t1/h0.04167 /h

    μ uses the natural log; doublings per hour uses log₂. They differ by a factor of ln 2 ≈ 0.693.

    QUESTIONS · DOUBLING TIME CALCULATOR

    Frequently asked

    How do I calculate cell doubling time?

    Doubling time = t × ln 2 ÷ ln(N ÷ N₀), where N₀ and N are the cell numbers at the start and end of an interval of length t. From 100,000 to 400,000 cells in 48 hours: 48 × 0.693 ÷ 1.386 = 24 hours.

    What is the difference between doubling time, population doublings and growth rate μ?

    Population doublings = log₂(N ÷ N₀): how many times the population doubled. Doubling time = time ÷ population doublings. The specific growth rate μ = ln(N ÷ N₀) ÷ t, in 1/h; μ = ln 2 ÷ doubling time. Doublings per hour (doublings ÷ t) is a different number from μ — it is μ ÷ ln 2.

    I split the cells during the interval. Can I still calculate doubling time?

    Yes, if you know the split exactly. Enter the split factor (4 for a 1:4 split, i.e. a quarter of the cells kept): the end count is multiplied by it. This assumes the split was exact and that no cells were lost — counting the cells at the split and starting a new row is more reliable.

    Should I use total cells or cells per mL?

    Total cells in the vessel. Cells/mL only works if the volume did not change between the two counts — feeding or topping up a suspension culture changes it.

    Why is my doubling time different from the published value?

    Doubling time depends on the medium, serum lot, seeding density, passage number and growth phase. Two counts give the average over the interval; if it includes the lag phase after seeding or the plateau at confluence, the value is longer than the log-phase doubling time. Count in mid-log phase for a value comparable to the literature.

    How accurate is a doubling time from two counts?

    It is only as good as the counts. With a small change in cell number the result is very sensitive: the tool shows how far the doubling time moves if each count is off by 10%, and flags intervals under 12 hours or less than a 1.5-fold change.

    How do I calculate cumulative population doublings over several passages?

    Add up the population doublings of each passage. Give the rows the same cell line name (a trailing passage number such as P14 is ignored) and the tool sums them per line, along with an overall doubling time (total time × ln 2 ÷ total ln fold change).

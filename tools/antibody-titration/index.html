        ABOUT THIS TOOL

        How to use

        1. Enter the ANTIBODY: the stock concentration from the vial and the amount the datasheet recommends per test.
        2. Set the SERIES: staining volume, how much cell suspension you add, where to start and the step. 4× the recommendation, 2-fold, 8 tubes is a common start.
        3. Choose SERIAL TRANSFER (one mix, carried tube to tube) or TUBE BY TUBE. Print the plan and tick the tubes off.
        4. Stain, acquire, and gate the positive and negative populations in each tube. Export the median of both and the robust SD (rSD) of the negative.
        5. Paste that table under STAIN INDEX. Check the columns and amounts the page picked.
        6. Save the amount to MY ANTIBODIES. The Staining Cocktail page uses it for every panel after.

        The formula

        SI = (MFIpos − MFIneg) ÷ (2 × rSDneg)
        MFImedian fluorescence of the positive and negative population
        rSDnegrobust SD of the negative population (SD also works)
        Separation(MFIpos − MFIneg) ÷ ((P84neg − MFIneg) ÷ 0.995)
        Mixeach tube's antibody mix is V ÷ (V − Vc) × the final concentration

        The page marks the highest index and the lowest amount within 90% of it.

        Example. 0.2 mg/mL PE antibody, 0.25 µg/test recommended, 100 µL with 50 µL cells, 4× start, 2-fold, 8 tubes, 10% spare: every tube needs 55 µL of mix. Tube 1 is 11 µL antibody + 99 µL buffer; 55 µL goes from tube to tube into 55 µL buffer. Amounts run 1 → 0.0078 µg/test (1:20 → 1:2560).

        Good to know

        1. Free to use. Nothing you paste leaves your browser.
        2. A titer holds for about the same number of cells in the same volume, at the same temperature and time.
        3. Titrate viability dyes and tandem dyes the same way; a new lot of a tandem is worth re-checking.
        4. A calculator, not a protocol: check your lab's method.

        QUESTIONS · ANTIBODY TITRATION

        Frequently asked

        What is the stain index?

        The difference between the positive and negative median fluorescence divided by twice the spread (SD, best the robust SD) of the negative population, as defined by Maecker et al., Cytometry A 2004. It rises with separation and falls when the negative population spreads.

        Which concentration should I pick?

        The page marks the amount with the highest index and the lowest amount within 90% of it. The lower one saves antibody and usually keeps background down; the best gives the widest separation.

        Where do I start the series?

        A common start is 2–4× the vendor's recommended amount in 2-fold steps over 8 tubes, which shows the saturation plateau and where the signal drops. Without a recommendation, 1–2 µg per 100 µL is a usual start.

        My table has no SD column. Can I still use it?

        Yes. With an 84th percentile of the negative population the page uses the separation index; with only medians it falls back to positive ÷ negative, which ignores spread. Export the robust SD from FlowJo to get the stain index.

        Does the titer hold for more cells or another volume?

        It holds for about the same cell number in the same staining volume. Staining many more cells, or in a different volume, needs the titration repeated or the amount scaled.

        Is my data uploaded?

        No. The table is read and worked out in your browser. Your antibody list is kept in this browser only; export it as a file to keep a copy.

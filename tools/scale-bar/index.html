02 — CALIBRATION ONE PER MICROSCOPE SET-UP

HOW

From your acquisition software or Fiji (Image › Properties), for the same objective, camera, binning and zoom as these images.

Select an image with a known length — a stage micrometer is best — and drag a line across it in the preview. Drag the ends to adjust; use the nudge buttons for single pixels.

START END

Measure across many divisions (e.g. 100 µm, not 10 µm): a one-pixel error then matters less.

Estimate only. µm/px = camera pixel × binning ÷ (objective × adapter or zoom). Real optics differ by a few percent — confirm with a stage micrometer before you publish.

SAVE AS PRESET

Presets are kept in this browser. A preset only holds for images taken with the same objective, camera, binning and zoom and exported at full size.

04 — EXPORT

FORMAT
16/32-BIT TIFF

PNG keeps every pixel as it was. JPG is smaller but re-compresses the image. TIFF files are saved as PNG. High-bit-depth TIFFs are mapped to 8 bits for display: MIN–MAX stretches each image’s own range; FULL RANGE maps 0 → black and the type’s maximum → white.

      Worked example

      Example. A 2048 × 2048 image from a 6.5 µm camera pixel with a 20× objective and no binning is 0.325 µm/px (a 666 µm field). AUTO picks 100 µm (≤ 20% of 666 µm), drawn as 308 px (307.69 exact, 0.1% off). With 2 × 2 binning the same field is 1024 px wide at 0.65 µm/px, and the 100 µm bar is 154 px.

      QUESTIONS · SCALE BAR

      Frequently asked

      Are my images uploaded?

      No. Images are opened and drawn on in your browser and never uploaded. Only your calibration presets and bar style are saved, on this device.

      Which image files can it open?

      PNG, JPG, WebP and BMP, plus TIFF: 8-, 16- and 32-bit greyscale, RGB and palette, uncompressed, LZW, PackBits or Deflate. For multi-page TIFF the first page is used. Proprietary formats such as .czi, .nd2, .lif and .oib need exporting from the microscope software or Fiji first.

      How do I find the µm per pixel of my images?

      Best: image a stage micrometer with the same objective, camera, binning and zoom, draw a line across a known distance and let the tool divide. Second best: the value your acquisition software or Fiji (Image › Properties) reports for that configuration.

      Can I work out the scale from the objective magnification alone?

      No. It also depends on the camera’s pixel size, binning, any adapter or zoom, and whether the image was resized after export. The camera method here estimates µm/px = camera pixel × binning ÷ total magnification; confirm it with a stage micrometer.

      Does it change my images?

      It draws the bar (and optional label and background box) onto a copy at the original pixel size. Nothing is resampled or adjusted. 16- and 32-bit TIFFs are converted to an 8-bit display copy, which is noted for each image.

      How long should a scale bar be?

      A round number (1, 2 or 5 × a power of ten) that spans roughly 10–25% of the image width. AUTO picks the largest round length up to 20% of the narrowest field in the batch, so every image gets the same bar.

      How many pixels long is a 50 µm scale bar?

      50 divided by the µm per pixel: 200 px at 0.25 µm/px, 154 px at 0.325 µm/px (153.85 rounded, 0.1% off). The tool shows the exact and rounded length and flags rounding above 1%.

      Can it read the scale saved by Fiji or ImageJ?

      Yes, from TIFFs saved by ImageJ / Fiji and from OME-TIFF PhysicalSizeX. It is shown as a suggestion; you choose whether to use it.

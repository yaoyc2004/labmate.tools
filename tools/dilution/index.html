    ABOUT THIS TOOL

    How to use

    1. Pick what you want to find under SOLVE FOR. Most of the time that is V1: how much stock to add.
    2. Type the other three values and pick their units. Units can differ — mM stock into µM final, mL into µL — the tool converts.
    3. Both concentrations must be the same kind: molar with molar, mass with mass, % with %, X with X. To go between molar and mg/mL, use the Molarity tool.
    4. The recipe shows how much stock and how much diluent (water, buffer or medium) to mix.
    5. If the stock volume is under 1 µL, you get a two-step plan with an intermediate dilution so you can pipette it accurately.
    6. COPY RECIPE puts the result on your clipboard, ready to paste into your notebook.

    The formula

    C1 × V1 = C2 × V2
    C1concentration of the stock
    V1volume of stock to take
    C2concentration you want
    V2total volume you want to end up with

    Diluent to add = V2 − V1. Dilution factor = C1 ÷ C2.

    Example. 1 mL of 10 µM from a 1 mM stock: V1 = 10 µM × 1 mL ÷ 1 mM = 10 µL. Add 10 µL stock to 990 µL diluent — a 1:100 dilution.

    Good to know

    1. Free to use. No subscription. Nothing you type leaves your browser.
    2. “1:100” here means 1 part stock in 100 parts total (1 + 99), the usual lab reading.
    3. Results are rounded to 4 significant figures.
    4. A calculator, not a protocol: check the numbers against your own method.
    QUESTIONS · DILUTION CALCULATOR

    Frequently asked

    How do I calculate how much stock to add?

    Use V₁ = C₂ × V₂ ÷ C₁. For 1 mL of 10 µM from a 1 mM stock: 10 µM × 1 mL ÷ 1 mM = 10 µL of stock plus 990 µL of diluent.

    Can I use different units for the stock and the final solution?

    Yes — mM into µM, or mL into µL, is converted for you. Both concentrations must be the same kind (molar with molar, mass with mass). To go between molar and mg/mL, use the Molarity Calculator.

    What if the stock volume is too small to pipette?

    When the stock volume is under 1 µL, the calculator gives a two-step plan with an intermediate dilution so you can pipette it accurately.

    What does a 1:100 dilution mean here?

    1 part stock in 100 parts total (1 + 99), the usual lab reading. The dilution factor is C₁ ÷ C₂.

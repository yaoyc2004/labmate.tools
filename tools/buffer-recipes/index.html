    ABOUT THIS TOOL

    How to use

    1. Pick a buffer. Every row shows its 1× composition; change any value to match your lab's recipe, or start from Custom recipe. Edits are kept in this browser.
    2. MAKE FROM POWDER: set the strength (10 for a 10× stock, 1 for working solution) and the volume. You get what to weigh or measure, the pH step, and the volume to make up to.
    3. DILUTE A STOCK: set the stock you have, the strength you want and the volume. You get the stock, any at-use additions (methanol, Tween) and the water.
    4. Hydrates and salt forms weigh more. Type the MW on your bottle.
    5. Single-component stocks that are a molarity or a %, without a recipe, are quicker in Molarity; plain C₁V₁ dilutions in Dilution.

    The formula

    m = c × N × V × MW
    mgrams of a powder
    cits concentration at 1×, mol/L
    Nstrength of what you make (10 for 10×)
    Vfinal volume, L

    % w/v: grams = % ÷ 100 × N × mL. % v/v: mL = % ÷ 100 × N × mL. From a stock: mL = c × N × V ÷ stock. Dilution: stock mL = V × wanted ÷ stock strength.

    Example. 1 L of 10× running buffer (1× = 25 mM Tris, 192 mM glycine, 0.1% SDS): Tris 0.025 × 10 × 1 × 121.14 = 30.29 g, glycine 0.192 × 10 × 1 × 75.07 = 144.1 g, SDS 0.1 ÷ 100 × 10 × 1000 = 10 g.

    Good to know

    1. Free to use. Nothing you type leaves your browser.
    2. Dissolve in about 80% of the final volume, set the pH, then make up to the mark: the pH shifts a little on dilution.
    3. Recipes differ between labs and suppliers (e.g. glycine 190 or 192 mM). The built-in ones are listed below with their sources.
    4. A calculator, not a protocol: check against your lab's method.

    QUESTIONS · BUFFER RECIPES

    Frequently asked

    How do I make 1 L of 10× Tris-glycine-SDS running buffer?

    1× is 25 mM Tris, 192 mM glycine and 0.1% SDS. For 1 L of 10×: 30.29 g Tris base, 144.1 g glycine and 10 g SDS, dissolved and made up to 1 L. Do not adjust the pH; it is about 8.3.

    How do I dilute 10× buffer to 1×?

    Take one tenth of the final volume as stock and make up with water: 100 mL of 10× plus 900 mL water gives 1 L of 1×. For transfer buffer with 20% methanol: 100 mL 10× + 200 mL methanol + 700 mL water.

    Why is methanol or Tween not in the 10× stock?

    They are added when you make the working buffer. Methanol at 10× strength would be 200%, and Tween is easier to measure into the working solution. The page lists them under the dilution.

    My Na₂HPO₄ is a hydrate. What do I change?

    Type the molecular weight on your bottle in the MW box: anhydrous 141.96, dihydrate 177.99, heptahydrate 268.07, dodecahydrate 358.14 g/mol. The grams follow.

    Can I make my own recipe?

    Yes. Choose Custom recipe, or edit any built-in one: each row has a 1× concentration, whether it is a powder, a neat liquid or a stock solution, and its molecular weight or stock concentration. Your edits are kept in this browser.

    Why keep TBE at 5× rather than 10×?

    10× TBE precipitates on storage. Cold Spring Harbor Protocols recommends 5× as the more stable stock.

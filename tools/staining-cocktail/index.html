    ABOUT THIS TOOL

    How to use

    1. Enter the TUBES you stain, the extra to make, the final staining volume and how much cocktail goes into each tube (the rest is cells).
    2. List the ANTIBODIES with the amount per test from your titration or the datasheet: µL per test, µg per test (with the stock) or 1:X.
    3. Or add them from MY ANTIBODIES, or send the panel here from Panel Designer.
    4. Add Brilliant Stain Buffer when two or more BV, BUV or BB dyes share the tube.
    5. Print the sheet and tick each component as you add it. Buffer goes in first, antibodies after.

    The formula

    Vtotal = Vper test × n × (1 + extra)
    Vper testµL/test as given · V ÷ X for 1:X · µg ÷ stock (mg/mL = µg/µL)
    buffercocktail per tube − antibodies − Brilliant buffer − Fc block
    ntubes; extra as % or as whole tubes

    A volume under 0.5 µL is flagged with a predilution to use instead.

    Example. 12 tubes + 10% = 13.2. CD3 BV421 1 µL/test → 13.2 µL; CD4 PE 0.125 µg/test from 0.2 mg/mL → 0.625 µL/test → 8.25 µL; CD8 APC 1:200 in 100 µL → 0.5 µL/test → 6.6 µL; buffer 50 − 2.125 = 47.9 µL/test → 632 µL. 660 µL cocktail, 50 µL per tube.

    Good to know

    1. Free to use. Nothing you type leaves your browser.
    2. Make the cocktail fresh, keep it cold and dark, and add tandem dyes last.
    3. A calculator, not a protocol: check your lab's method.

    QUESTIONS · STAINING COCKTAIL

    Frequently asked

    How much extra should I make?

    10% or one or two extra tubes covers what stays in the tube and the tips. With many tubes a percentage is fairer; with three or four tubes add a whole tube.

    Why is a volume flagged under 0.5 µL?

    Most pipettes are not accurate below about 0.5 µL. Predilute that antibody in staining buffer (the page gives the dilution and volumes) or make the cocktail for more tubes.

    Do I need Brilliant Stain Buffer?

    When a panel has two or more Brilliant polymer dyes (BV, BUV, BB and similar), they can stick to each other and give false double-positive events. The buffer prevents it. Check the volume per test on its datasheet.

    Can the viability dye go into the cocktail?

    Fixable (amine-reactive) viability dyes bind any protein, including the BSA or serum in staining buffer. Stain with them in protein-free PBS first, wash, then add the cocktail. The page lists them in their own table.

    What does 1:X mean here?

    1:200 means 1 part antibody in 200 parts of the final staining volume: 0.5 µL in 100 µL. It is the dilution in the tube, not in the cocktail.

    Where do the amounts come from?

    Type them from your titration or the datasheet, or add antibodies saved on the Antibody Titration page. A titer saved at another staining volume is scaled to keep the same concentration.

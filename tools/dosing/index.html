    The sheet has the animals with their volumes in large type, check boxes (one per day for a multi-day course), time and initials columns, the preparation recipe and sign-off lines. Animals with a flag are marked ⚠. Your approved protocol's doses and volumes take precedence over this tool.

    ABOUT THIS TOOL

    How to use

    1. Weigh the animals and paste the list: ID, cage, group and weight in grams. One group is fine — leave the Group column out.
    2. Pick the species and route. Under I KNOW THE, choose CONCENTRATION if your solution is already made (or the concentration is fixed), or mL/kg TO GIVE if you dose at a fixed volume such as 10 mL/kg and need the concentration to make.
    3. Type each group's dose. A dose of 0 makes a vehicle group.
    4. Pick the syringe you will use. Volumes are rounded to its graduations, and the change in dose is shown.
    5. Set the vehicle, days and overage to get the recipe: how much drug to weigh (or stock to take) and each vehicle component, in order.
    6. Fix anything marked in CHECK, fill in the study details and print the sheet.

    The formula

    V = D × W ÷ C
    Vvolume to give (mL)
    Ddose (mg/kg)
    Wbody weight (kg) = grams ÷ 1000
    Cworking concentration (mg/mL)

    Fixed dose volume v (mL/kg): C = D ÷ v and V = v × W. Every animal in a group gets the same mL/kg = D ÷ C.

    To prepare: (sum of rounded volumes + dead volume × animals) × doses × (1 + overage). Drug to weigh = C × that volume × salt factor ÷ purity.

    Example. A 25.0 g mouse, tamoxifen 75 mg/kg IP from 20 mg/mL in corn oil: 75 × 0.025 = 1.875 mg, ÷ 20 = 0.094 mL (3.75 mL/kg, well under the 20 mL/kg good-practice IP volume). On a 1 mL syringe that is 0.09 mL, 4.0% under the dose; on a 0.3 mL insulin syringe, 0.095 mL, 1.3% over.

    Good to know

    1. Free to use. Nothing you type leaves your browser. Settings (not the animals) are remembered on this device.
    2. Weigh again on each dosing day and print a fresh sheet; the recipe for a multi-day course uses today's weights plus the overage.
    3. The tool does not judge solubility, stability, pH, osmolality or toxicity.
    4. A calculator, not a protocol: your IACUC-approved doses and volumes come first.

    QUESTIONS · RODENT DOSING SHEET

    Frequently asked

    How do I calculate the injection volume for a mouse from a mg/kg dose?

    Volume (mL) = dose (mg/kg) × body weight (kg) ÷ concentration (mg/mL). A 25 g mouse at 75 mg/kg from a 20 mg/mL solution: 75 × 0.025 ÷ 20 = 0.094 mL (94 µL).

    What is the maximum injection volume for a mouse or rat?

    Diehl et al. (2001) give good-practice volumes with possible maximums in brackets. Mouse: oral 10 (50) mL/kg, SC 10 (40), IP 20 (80), IV bolus 5, slow IV up to 25, IM 0.05 (0.1) mL per site. Rat: oral 10 (40), SC 5 (10), IP 10 (20), IV bolus 5, slow IV up to 20, IM 0.1 (0.2) mL per site. Your approved protocol and your institution's guidelines take precedence.

    My institution's limits are different. Can I use them?

    Yes. Choose MY INSTITUTION under volume limits and type your own good-practice and maximum values per route. They are kept in your browser and printed on the dosing sheet.

    Why round the volume to the syringe?

    You can only draw what the syringe shows. The tool rounds each volume to the nearest graduation and shows how much that changes the dose. If the change is over 5% or the volume is under two graduations, it suggests a finer syringe or a more dilute solution.

    How much solution should I prepare?

    The sum of the rounded volumes for every animal, times the number of doses, plus overage (20% by default) and any dead volume per animal for the needle hub. For daily dosing, weigh the animals again each day and print a new sheet.

    How do I make 10% DMSO, 40% PEG300, 5% Tween-80, 45% saline?

    For 1 mL: dissolve the drug in 100 µL DMSO (or use a DMSO stock), add 400 µL PEG300 and mix, add 50 µL Tween-80 and mix, then add 450 µL saline. If you use a DMSO stock, the stock counts toward the 10% DMSO.

    Does the calculator check solubility, stability or toxicity?

    No. It does the arithmetic and checks volumes. Whether the compound dissolves, stays stable in the vehicle, or is safe at that dose has to come from the literature, the supplier and your approved protocol.

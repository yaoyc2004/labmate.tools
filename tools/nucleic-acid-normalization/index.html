    ABOUT THIS TOOL

    How to use

    1. Copy the results table from the NanoDrop or Qubit software (or Excel) and paste it into box 1 — or open the exported file. The page finds the name, concentration, unit and ratio columns itself and says what it read.
    2. No export? Type one sample per line: a name, then ng/µL, and optionally 260/280 and 260/230.
    3. Measured a sample twice? Tick Average repeat readings and readings with the same name are averaged; the spread is shown.
    4. Pick a set-up, or type the amount per tube and the final volume. CONCENTRATION mode makes every tube the same ng/µL instead (e.g. 20 ng/µL for sequencing).
    5. Read the sheet: green, amber and red mark the purity ratios; rows that need a pre-dilution or are too dilute say what to do.
    6. PRINT SHEET for the bench (tick each tube as you go), EXCEL or CSV for your notebook, or a worklist for a liquid handler. Tick boxes on screen work too and are kept in this browser.

    The formula

    Vsample = m ÷ csample
    diluentVfinal − Vsample
    by concentrationm = cfinal × Vfinal
    too diluteVsample > Vfinal: use Vfinal, gives c × Vfinal
    pre-dilutionVsample < vmin: dilute 1:k first, add k × Vsample

    Example. 1 µg RNA in 10 µL, sample at 412.6 ng/µL: 1000 ÷ 412.6 = 2.42 µL sample + 7.58 µL water. At 2650 ng/µL it would be 0.38 µL — under 0.5 µL — so dilute 2 µL + 6 µL water (1:4) and add 1.51 µL of that.

    Good to know

    1. Free to use. No subscription. Nothing you paste or open leaves your browser.
    2. Add the diluent to the tube first, then the sample, so a small volume goes into liquid and is mixed in rather than left on the wall.
    3. Absorbance (NanoDrop) counts every nucleotide, free ones and RNA in a DNA prep included; a dye assay (Qubit) counts only its target. For library prep, normalize on the Qubit value.
    4. Volumes are rounded to steps you can set on a pipette; GIVES shows the amount after rounding.

    QUESTIONS · DNA & RNA NORMALIZATION

    Frequently asked

    How do I calculate how much RNA to add for 1 µg in a reverse transcription?

    Sample volume = 1000 ng ÷ concentration in ng/µL; water makes up the rest of the template volume. RNA at 412.6 ng/µL needs 2.42 µL, plus 7.58 µL water for 10 µL.

    What are good 260/280 and 260/230 ratios?

    About 1.8 for DNA and 2.0 for RNA at 260/280; 260/230 is usually 2.0–2.2. Lower 260/280 suggests protein or phenol; lower 260/230 suggests guanidine salt, phenol or carbohydrate carry-over. Below about 10 ng/µL the ratios are too noisy to judge.

    Which NanoDrop and Qubit files can I paste?

    Exports from NanoDrop One/OneC, Eight, 8000 and 2000/Lite, and Qubit 4 and Qubit Flex (.csv, .tsv, .txt or .xlsx), a block copied from Excel, or a plain list with a name and ng/µL on each line. For Qubit the original sample concentration is used, not the tube concentration.

    What if a sample is too concentrated to pipette?

    When the volume falls under your smallest pipetting volume (0.5 µL by default) the sheet tells you to pre-dilute it, for example 2 µL + 18 µL water (1:10), and gives the volume of the diluted sample to add.

    What if a sample is too dilute?

    It is flagged and the whole final volume is used, with the amount it actually gives. The page also tells you the largest amount every sample can reach, so you can lower the target for all of them.

    Is my data uploaded?

    No. Pasting, opening files, the calculation and the exports all happen in your browser.

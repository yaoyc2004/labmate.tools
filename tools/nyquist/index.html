    ABOUT THIS TOOL

    How to use

    1. Choose the MODE: widefield, point-scanning confocal, spinning disk or two-photon.
    2. Pick the OBJECTIVE by brand and model, or type magnification, NA and immersion from the barrel.
    3. Pick the CAMERA (widefield, spinning disk) or the CONFOCAL with your frame size and zoom. Or tick I already know the pixel size.
    4. Add your CHANNELS. The strictest one (shortest wavelength) sets the target.
    5. Read the RESULT: pixel size against Nyquist, the adapter, binning or zoom that reaches it, and the z-step. Print the card for the microscope.

    The formulas

    Δx = λ ÷ (4k · n sin α) · Δz = λ ÷ (2k · n (1 − cos α))
    αhalf-aperture angle, sin α = NA ÷ n
    Widefieldk = 1, λ = emission
    Confocal, diskk = 2, λ = excitation
    Two-photonk = 2, λ = the infrared excitation
    Pixelcamera: pixel × binning ÷ (M × fTL/fd × Mc); confocal: (F ÷ √2) ÷ (M × zoom × N)

    Resolution shown is the Rayleigh criterion (0.61 λ ÷ NA widefield; the pinhole-dependent confocal form).

    Example. 60×/1.42 oil, ORCA-Fusion (6.5 µm), 1× adapter, EGFP (507 nm emission), widefield: pixel 108 nm, Nyquist 89 nm — undersampled 1.21×. A 1.5× adapter gives 72 nm, at Nyquist. Same objective on a confocal at 1024 × 1024 with a 20 mm scan field needs zoom 5.4 for the 43 nm target at 488 nm.

    Good to know

    1. Free to use. Nothing leaves your browser.
    2. Nyquist is the coarsest sampling that keeps all the resolution; finer costs photons, time and file size.
    3. Real pixel sizes differ from nominal by a few percent. Calibrate with a stage micrometer before you measure.
    4. Hardware values come from the makers' public spec sheets; check yours.

    QUESTIONS · NYQUIST SAMPLING

    Frequently asked

    What pixel size do I need for Nyquist sampling?

    For widefield, the pixel on the sample should be at most λem ÷ (4 NA); for a confocal, λex ÷ (8 NA). With NA 1.4 and GFP that is about 91 nm widefield and 44 nm confocal. The page works it out per channel and uses the strictest.

    My objective or camera is not in the list. Can I still use it?

    Yes. Every field can be typed: magnification, NA, immersion, camera pixel size, frame size, zoom, wavelengths. The lists only fill the numbers in. Use SUBMIT TO THE LIBRARY to send us your model so it can be added for everyone.

    Is undersampling always bad?

    No. You lose resolution you paid for in NA, but you gain field of view, speed and signal per pixel. For deconvolution, SVI suggests staying within about 1.3× (widefield) to 1.6× (confocal at 1 Airy unit) of the Nyquist distance.

    Why does the tube lens matter?

    Infinity objectives are designed for one tube lens: 200 mm for Nikon and Leica, 180 mm for Evident and 165 mm for Zeiss. On another maker's stand the real magnification is the nominal one × stand tube lens ÷ design tube lens.

    Where do the hardware specifications come from?

    From each maker's public specification pages, datasheets and core-facility documents, linked per entry in the table below. Entries marked check have a number still to confirm. The list is independent and not sponsored.

    Is anything uploaded?

    No. Everything is worked out in your browser. Your own objectives and cameras are kept in this browser; export them to keep a copy.
